This was a **4 days 3 nights** trip with **ten people**: two older relatives, two toddlers, and the rest of us in between. A group that size does not move like a couple with a daypack. Meals, toilets, naps, and Grab vans matter more than a tight itinerary.

We split into two groups for the flights. Day 1 below is from our side — two adults and two toddlers, flying Thai Airways from Singapore Changi. From Day 2 the whole group is together.

## Before you fly

Take a few minutes and fill in the [Thai Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/arrival-card/#/home) before you leave. It is the official form, and there is no fee.

Do not be alarmed by the aliens inside the website. 🙂

## Day 1 — Changi to Sathorn, then CentralWorld

Thai Airways served lunch on the tray: rice with a stew, a small salad, bread with Orchid butter, and a layered dessert. It was ordinary airline food, which is all we needed with two toddlers in the row.

### Grab at Suvarnabhumi

Baggage claim is on **Level 2**. For Grab, go down to **Level 1**.

Suvarnabhumi does not work like Grab in Singapore. After you book, you do not get a driver name, ETA, and plate number. You get a **pin code**.

Then:

1. Exit the airport and walk toward the large Grab sign. You will pass the ordinary taxi and coach lanes.
2. Queue at Grab with that pin. Staff will help if you show them the code.
3. When you board, the driver asks for the pin again. After they enter it, they see your destination.

With toddlers and luggage, budget extra time for that walk and the queue. It is organised, but it is not a kerbside pickup at the door.

We reached the hotel around **4:45pm**. Street view on the way in was typical Sathorn: overcast sky, a pedestrian overpass, a tangle of overhead wires, and Bangrak Medical Center on Sathon Tai Road.

### 7-Eleven, then CentralWorld

Everyone was hungry, and we had older relatives and toddlers in tow. A few of us walked to a nearby **7-Eleven** and brought food back to the rooms.

Thailand’s 7-Eleven is not a Singapore convenience store with extra snacks. There is a wide range of food, they will heat it for you, and many branches have a barista counter as well. For a family landing mid-afternoon, it is the fastest way to eat without committing to a restaurant.

By the time we had eaten, it was past **6pm**. We booked a Grab van to **CentralWorld**.

The mall is huge. The original plan was **Haidilao**. The wait was **one hour**. With the group we had, that was too long, so we went to a nearby food court for local food instead.

The food court runs on a stored-value card. You top up at a machine and it prints a receipt. When you are done, take the same receipt back to the machine and refund whatever is left. Do not throw the slip away at the table.

We also stopped at **KHIRI Thai Tea** — ceramic teapots on backlit shelves, and drinks that look like they belong on a review more than a food-court tray: frozen Thai tea, a thick layer of orange shavings, cream on top.

If you are at CentralWorld with women in the group, **NaRaYa** is worth a visit. You can buy original bags at proper quality here, rather than guessing at a night market.

We also queued for **Karen coffee**. It was late; the stall had **seven cups** left and we were **fifth in line**, so they would only sell us **one**. We bought it anyway and shared it so everyone could taste it.

Shops were closing, so we Grabbed back to the hotel, made a last 7-Eleven run for supper, and called it a day.

## Day 2 — Erawan Shrine, Siam, then ICONSIAM

Breakfast was at the hotel. First stop was **Erawan Shrine** (Thao Maha Phrom / Phra Phrom), at the Ratchaprasong intersection. You can take the BTS there. With ten of us, we booked a Grab van instead.

We came to pray for career, family, love, and the children. The shrine is four-faced. Walk **clockwise** from the entrance and pray at all four sides:

| Order (clockwise from the entrance) | Side |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Career (faces the entrance) |
| 2 | Love |
| 3 | Wealth (the back) |
| 4 | Health |

We did the usual round of prayer and put some notes in as an act of kindness. If you make a wish and it comes true, the custom is to come back and repay it by hiring the dancers who perform in front of the shrine.

From Erawan you can walk to **Siam Paragon** on the connecting bridge. No need to get back in a van for that stretch.

The mall was packed around the food. We split across tables in the basement to eat whatever we could get. Once we were done, we walked to **Nextopia**, which is inside Siam Paragon. Entrance is free, and it is an easy place to bring kids. The basement of Nextopia has a few restaurants and, when we passed, it was relatively empty. We should have had lunch there. Our stomachs were already full, so we skipped it.

There is a **MELAND** indoor playground inside Nextopia. Check the boards at the door before you commit — with a large family the numbers add up quickly. When we visited, a small toddler was about **860 THB** for three hours, older kids about **1,380 THB**, a parent **280 THB**, and non-slip socks **80 THB** a pair (required). Overtime was **90 THB** per 30 minutes. All-day tickets were single entry and non-refundable. Prices change; treat the photo as what the screens said that day.

We found a **Pop Mart** and tried our luck on the Coffee Factory series. My wife wanted the Crybaby figure. After comparing the boxes, the Crybaby felt slightly heavier than the other three. We estimated by hand, picked that one, and hit it.

Next was a Grab van to **ICONSIAM**. Traffic was heavy. We sat in the van for almost **two hours**. Long enough that the Grab app decided the driver might be making unnecessary stops and asked whether I wanted to report it to the police. I did not. The jam inched forward, we crossed the bridge, and we arrived.

The basement at ICONSIAM is Thai boat-and-street food, and it was crowded. With the group we had, we went upstairs for a restaurant instead.

We sat at **Laem Charoen Seafood**. There was not much of a queue, and the food was genuinely good. The waitress worked hard to look after us even though her English was limited. You could see the effort: warm, a little brave, and she made sure the orders landed.

Inside the mall there is a light-and-laser waterfall between **Level 4 and Level 7**. Worth a photo if you are already there.

We then looked for **Japara** perfume. There is no official store in ICONSIAM; the official one is at CentralWorld. My mother-in-law wanted a car fragrance pouch for her nephew. We did not find the scent he wanted, so we gave up and did supermarket shopping in the basement instead.

Closing time arrived while we were still in the mall. Pickup is at the coach and taxi area: narrow, humid, warm, and full of people waiting for the same vans. After a wait, our Grab van showed up and we went back to the hotel.

## Day 3 — Samyan, Cloud 11, Song Wat, then Jodd Fairs

The plan for the morning was a local wet market. We picked **Samyan Market**.

I wanted to try the BTS properly, so I went on my own: National Stadium station, then a **22-minute walk**. The stretch in between is greener than the malls — parked scooters under trees, a blue sports court behind a fence, then a wide paved plaza with shade.

We walked over to **Samyan Mitrtown**. It has a Big C, MUJI, and Chagee. We were too early: most eateries were still closed. We ended up at **The Coffee Club**. It is a decent cafe. Not every waitress spoke English. That was manageable — Google Translate, or they fetch the colleague who can.

Next was **Cloud 11**, a new mall. Most units still had no tenants. Level 2 was given over to exhibitions: no entrance fee, walk around, take photos. We found a cafe where a DJ was playing rock while the barista brewed. It is a good mall if you have small children. They can run on Level 1 while you sit on a bench and watch.

Then **Song Wat**, the old street near Chinatown. Narrow lanes, wet ground, string lights, cafes. Street-art photos are easy. Finding a toilet is not. It was crowded with tourists.

## Day 4 — 7-Eleven, then Suvarnabhumi

I would not call this a full day. The flight was **1:50pm local time**, so we had to leave the hotel at **10:30am**. We bought quick food at 7-Eleven, let the children eat in the room, then took a Grab van to Suvarnabhumi.

For a group of ten, that is the right last morning: no extra temple, no extra mall, no second pin-code adventure if you can avoid it.

## What I would repeat

Bangkok with toddlers and older relatives works if you treat **Grab vans**, **7-Eleven**, and **mall food courts** as infrastructure, not as failures of taste. The shrine, the markets, and Song Wat are worth doing. The long jam to ICONSIAM is the thing I would plan around next time — leave earlier, or stay on the BTS side of the river until later in the day.
