I opened [Bedtime Pals](https://bedtimepals.defiq.app/) the way a parent would on a school night: phone in portrait, lights low, one story from the cover through the last page. The date of this note is **23 October 2026**. The catalogue that evening held **sixteen** illustrated stories, all marked **ages 5 to 7**, in **British English**, most of them a **7 to 9 minute** listen.

This is early primary, the kindergarten to Primary 1 / Key Stage 1 band. The stories are longer than a toddler board book, and they are written to be heard aloud.

## Quick take

Bedtime Pals is a free, mobile-first story reader from Defiq Technologies. The homepage keeps a short "latest" shelf and sends the rest of the library to a searchable list. Each story has a cover, a short preview, and a full-bleed reader: picture edge to edge, cream text, a gold word highlight while the voice is on.

You can read it yourself. Or you tap **Read Story** and a recorded voice takes the page. The full narration waits for that tap. A cover preview can play on its own only after a parent ticks **Auto-play preview on story covers**.

**Sleep sounds** sit in the reader as an option you turn on: soft music and rain, separate sliders, and the mix drops a little while the story is read aloud. The wind-down is story first, then a quiet room, with the adult still holding the buttons.

## What a night with it actually is

I read *Somebody Called Shh* in the reader. Ten pages. The opening is already a person with a job:

> I am Scraps, and I do the stairs. Every night, when Dad pulls the shutter down on Lo's Fish Bar and the fryer stops humming through the floor, I do the count. Nose by nose.

Dad smells of vinegar and salt. Mum smells of hand cream. The cat hisses, and that still counts. A new baby arrives who smells of nothing at all, and Scraps decides a baby must be a kind of fish, because they live above a chip shop. That is the standard the rest of the shelf is aiming at: a want on page one, one object that keeps coming back (here, a rubber fish finger), a small cost, something you can smell, and an ending that feels like bed rather than a lecture.

The other covers on the homepage the same night were in the same register. *No Tiptoes* (about 9 minutes, posted 23 Oct 2026), *Stop on the Good Bit*, *Same O'Clock*, *Green for Go*, *One for Next*. Read counts on the cards were still in single digits. This is a young library, not a household name.

## The English, and the 9 out of 10 bar

The prose is British on purpose: spellings and cadence aimed at readers in the UK and Singapore, sentences that survive being said out loud. The house rule is that a draft is not finished until an independent reader scores it **9 out of 10**, and narration is recorded only after that pass, because each recording costs money. *Somebody Called Shh* is the piece they revise toward.

That 9 out of 10 is a workshop gate. It is the score they refuse to publish a story under. It is not a critic's star rating sitting on the cover, and it is not a claim that every listener has graded the catalogue. What you can check yourself is the page: concrete nouns, a joke, a child (or a dog) who is briefly unreasonable, and no sentence that stops to announce the moral.

## Sound, without the voice taking over

Two sound choices, and they are separate.

1. **Narration.** Tap **Read Story** on the page you want. The voice does not start when the reader opens.
2. **Sleep sounds.** Music volume and rain, off until you switch the background on. The note under the sliders says the sound lowers a little while the story is read aloud, so the voice stays in front.

Parents who want a soundtrack after the last line can leave the rain on, put the phone face down, and keep the room dark. That is a settling ritual. It is not a sleep study, and this review does not claim that children who listen sleep better. The product is built so the last minutes of the day are calm, short, and finished.

## Who it suits

Use it if the child is about five to seven and still wants a proper story at lights-out, with pictures, and with the option of a calm voice when you are too tired to perform one.

- One story a night, about the length of a chapter, then stop.
- British English you are happy for them to hear (mum, colour, the chip shop, the coach station).
- A parent who wants to decide when any voice starts.

Skip it if you need board books for toddlers, or a huge back catalogue tonight. Sixteen stories is a start. New ones arrive often enough that the homepage asks for an email when the next night is published. There is also an advertisement on the page. It is a free site, and the ad is visible.

## What I would still watch

The ambition in the workshop is high. The public record is still small: a handful of reads, a handful of likes, and a catalogue measured in dozens, not hundreds. Early stories in the same project were, by the makers' own notes, too alike and too solemn, which is why the later ones work harder at a joke and a child who gets something wrong. Read *Somebody Called Shh* first. If that voice is the one you want at bedtime, the rest of the shelf is built to the same brief.
